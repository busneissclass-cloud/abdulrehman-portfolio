Case study

EcoFold
Packaging.

E-COMMERCE · LIVE

01

The challenge.

EcoFold Packaging sells eco-friendly packaging to conscious brands — boxes, mailers, cosmetics packaging and food packaging. In this market, buyers are usually procurement teams and small business owners who research suppliers online before they ever pick up the phone. That puts the store in a very specific position: the website is not just a catalogue, it is the salesperson, the showroom and the first impression all at once.

The challenge had two sharp edges. First, product discovery. Packaging buyers think in product lines and use cases — a cosmetics brand needs different packaging than a food delivery business — so the store had to organise a wide catalogue in a way that lets a buyer find the right product in seconds, not minutes. Slow, confusing navigation kills commercial intent fast. Second, timing. A buyer who has found the right product is hot right now; every extra step between "this is what I need" and "here is my quote request" is a place where the deal can cool off and walk to a competitor.

Underneath both was a third, quieter challenge: search demand. Packaging buyers search Google for exactly what they need — "eco-friendly mailers", "custom cosmetic boxes" and thousands of variations. A store that is not built to capture that demand from day one is leaving its cheapest source of high-intent traffic on the table.

02

Strategy.

The strategy was simple to state and disciplined to execute: build the store around the buyer's journey, not around the catalogue. Every decision was measured against three questions — does this help a buyer find the product faster, does it move them closer to a quote, and can Google find and understand it?

Product lines became the primary organising principle. Instead of one long, undifferentiated catalogue, the store groups packaging by line — boxes, mailers, cosmetics, food — matching how buyers actually think about their needs. A visitor lands, recognises their category immediately and is two clicks from a product that fits. Category and product pages were structured with clean, descriptive URLs and heading hierarchies, so the same architecture that serves buyers also serves search engines.

The second strategic pillar was the quote flow. In B2B packaging, most buyers are not buying on impulse; they need custom quantities, materials and pricing. So the store treats the quote request as the primary conversion, not the checkout. An instant quote flow sits close to every product, catching buyers while their intent is at its peak and making it effortless to start a conversation.

Finally, SEO was treated as a launch requirement, not a phase two. Metadata, schema, page speed and crawl structure were baked into the build, because retrofitting SEO after launch means paying twice — once to build, once to fix.

03

Execution.

Storefront architecture. Product lines for boxes, mailers, cosmetics and food packaging were set up as distinct, browsable categories, each with its own landing surface. The hierarchy stays shallow — no product is buried more than a couple of clicks from the homepage — because depth is where conversions go to die.

Instant quote flow. A fast, low-friction quote request sits alongside the product content. The form asks for only what is needed to quote: product, quantity, contact details. No account creation, no multi-page wizard. The goal is simple: turn a hot visitor into a named enquiry in under a minute.

SEO foundations. Clean URL structure, descriptive title tags and meta descriptions per category and product, proper heading order, product and organisation schema, compressed images and lean assets for fast mobile load times. The site speaks to Google in the same language it speaks to buyers.

Mobile-first and tracked. Designed for the phone first — most B2B research now starts on mobile — and shipped with analytics and Search Console wired up before launch day, so every enquiry, quote request and traffic source is measurable from week one.

Handover. The business owns everything: domain, store, data and logins, with documentation the team can actually use. Nothing held hostage.

05

Outcome.

The objective was never a vanity metric — it was a working sales asset. The store was built to do three jobs: rank for the packaging queries its buyers actually search, turn product-page visitors into quote requests with minimal friction, and give the business clean data on what is working from the first week.

Every structural choice serves that objective. The product-line architecture means a buyer who lands from search arrives on a page that matches their intent. The instant quote flow means intent is captured while it is hot. The SEO and tracking foundations mean the business can see which categories and queries produce enquiries and invest accordingly. I will not invent traffic or revenue figures — but the site exists for one reason: to make it easier to find, easier to quote, and easier to buy than the alternative.
